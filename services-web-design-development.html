Supporting build capability

Web & digital experience when the system needs a better front door.

HJT builds or modernizes websites when the customer journey, lead system, application architecture, or secure digital experience requires it. The website is treated as one layer in the operating system — not as a separate agency offer competing for attention with HJT's core services.

What's actually happening

Most business websites are still disconnected from the operation behind them.

A site can look polished and still create operational failure: leads enter the wrong inbox, forms do not reach the CRM, attribution disappears, sensitive data is collected without a clear purpose, or the handoff after submission depends on someone noticing an email. HJT treats the digital front door as part of the larger system.

What gets built

A digital experience connected to the business behind it.

Conversion-focused experience

  • Built around what the visitor actually needs to do next and how that action should move into the business.

CRM & workflow integration

  • Forms, booking, qualification, and customer actions connect to the systems responsible for ownership and follow-up.

Security & data handling

  • Collect only what the workflow needs, route it intentionally, and avoid creating unnecessary exposure or unclear ownership.

Analytics & observability

  • Measure what visitors do, where journeys fail, and whether the downstream system actually receives and acts on the event.
Where it fits

Built when the architecture calls for it.

Web and digital-experience work is usually part of a broader customer, revenue, automation, application, or systems engagement. HJT can still scope a focused website project when that is genuinely the problem, but the company does not lead its market position as a general web-design agency.

Before you book

What people actually ask.

Can HJT still build a website?

Yes. The capability remains available. The difference is positioning: HJT treats the site as part of a larger business system rather than presenting website design as one of several equal core services.

Will it work with my existing CRM or tools?

Where the integrations and access model make sense, yes. HJT starts by mapping what should happen after a visitor takes action, then connects the necessary systems around that workflow.

What if the website is the only thing that needs fixing?

Then a focused project may still be appropriate. HJT will not force a larger architecture engagement when the evidence shows the problem is genuinely limited to the digital front door.
