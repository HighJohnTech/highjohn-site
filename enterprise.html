Who this is for

The economics of the department have to justify the transformation.

Revenue alone is not the qualification. HJT looks for organizations where a department controls meaningful payroll, customer throughput, revenue, capacity, or operational risk — and where the current system can be measured before and after change.

01

Material operating scale

A real team, repeated workflows, meaningful customer or transaction volume, and enough operating consequence to justify redesign.

02

Executive ownership

An owner, CEO, COO, CRO, department leader, or accountable sponsor who can define the outcome and remove blockers.

03

Measurable opportunity

Current-state evidence around time, capacity, conversion, reliability, cost, quality, security, or customer experience.

Engagement path

Diagnose the department before promising the transformation.

01

Executive fit

Confirm the problem, sponsor, operating stakes, department boundary, and whether HJT is the right partner.

02

Department intelligence assessment

Interview leadership and frontline stakeholders, map systems and workflows, establish baselines, constraints, risks, and a prioritized future-state architecture.

03

Transformation

Implement the approved architecture across AI, automation, integrations, software, controls, instrumentation, training, and operating handoffs.

04

Managed optimization

Monitor reliability, improve workflows, review permissions, control model/tool costs, expand what works, and keep the system current.

Security-led transformation

AI does not get unlimited access just because automation is possible.

HJT designs data boundaries, permissions, human approval gates, logging, failure handling, rollback, and escalation alongside the business workflow so the transformation is governable after launch.
