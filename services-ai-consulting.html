What's actually happening

Everyone's told to "use AI." Almost nobody's told where.

Most businesses end up with a scattering of AI tools bought on trend — a chatbot here, a writing assistant there — none of it connected, none of it solving the actual bottleneck. The problem was never a lack of AI. It's a lack of a plan for where AI creates real leverage versus where it's just noise.

What gets built

A plan before a purchase.

AI opportunity assessment

  • An honest read on where AI would actually move the needle in your business — and where it wouldn't.

Architecture & tool selection

  • Choosing what to build versus what to buy, and how it should all connect — before any tool gets purchased.

Implementation planning

  • A real sequence and timeline for rolling AI into the business — not a vague roadmap that never gets executed.

Hands-on mentorship

  • Working directly with founders and teams so the knowledge stays in-house, not locked in a consultant's head.
How it's positioned

Business problem first. AI second.

We don't start with a cool piece of AI technology and go looking for somewhere to use it. We start with what's actually broken or slow in the business, and only bring AI into the plan where it genuinely helps — sometimes the honest answer is that AI isn't the right fix yet, and we'll say so.

Before you book

What people actually ask.

Yes — most clients start exactly there. The point of the assessment is to translate "I don't know where to start" into an actual plan, in plain language.

No. If the honest recommendation is "don't touch AI yet, fix this process first," that's what you'll hear. The goal is leverage, not a tool list.

Does this lead into a bigger build?

It can, if that's the right next step — but the consulting engagement stands on its own and isn't a sales funnel into anything larger.
